# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: A Literature Survey

## TL;DR

- Efficient inference has converged on four levers — quantization, KV-cache management, speculative/parallel decoding, and serving-system scheduling — with post-training quantization (PTQ) as the single most widely adopted technique [1][2][3][4][5].
- Compression is not free: KV-cache compression and aggressive quantization measurably degrade long-context, multi-instruction, and safety behaviour, so "efficiency" claims must be paired with behavioural evaluation [6][7][8][9][10].
- Small language models are now defined by deployment context rather than a hard parameter cutoff, and the dominant training recipe is "small model, far more data than compute-optimal" — SmolLM2 is a 1.7B model overtrained on roughly 11T tokens [11][12][13][14].
- Inference-aware scaling laws imply that when inference demand is high, models should be strictly smaller and trained longer than Chinchilla-optimal, which is the economic rationale for the current SLM wave [14][15][16].
- The serving stack has moved from kernel-level tricks to distributed scheduling problems: chunked prefill, prefill/decode disaggregation, and phase-aware scheduling dominate recent throughput results [17][18][19].
- Reported efficiency gains often do not survive contact with production: theoretical speculative-decoding bounds overstate real speedups, and fleets over-provision context for requests that are mostly short [20][21].

## Background

"Efficient inference" refers to the set of techniques that reduce the memory, latency, energy or cost of running a language model, without (ideally) changing its output distribution. The field's canonical framing comes from roofline-style analysis: LLM inference is split into a compute-bound *prefill* phase that processes the prompt and a memory-bandwidth-bound *decode* phase that generates one token at a time [22][23]. Because decode repeatedly streams the whole model and the whole KV cache for each token, memory traffic — not arithmetic — is usually the binding constraint, which is why compression techniques (which shrink what must be moved) dominate the literature [22][24][25].

"Small language models" (SLMs) are compact models intended to run under resource constraints — on-device, at the edge, or in cost-sensitive cloud deployments — where latency, privacy and infrastructure cost favour local execution [11][12][26]. Survey after survey notes that there is still no consensus parameter cutoff for "small," with definitions ranging from sub-billion models up to roughly 15B parameters [11][12][27][26]. The two literatures are tightly coupled: SLMs are the main *target* of compression and distillation work, and efficient-inference methods are what make small models usable in production [25][28][29].

## Quantization: the dominant compression lever

Post-training quantization (PTQ) is the most widely deployed efficiency technique, and it splits into weight-only and weight-plus-activation schemes. GPTQ performs one-shot weight quantization using approximate second-order information and demonstrated 175B-scale inference on a single GPU [1]; AWQ instead protects the salient weight channels identified through activation statistics, yielding hardware-friendly low-bit weights with better generalization than prior methods [2]. Going further down the bit-width ladder, SmoothQuant migrates activation outliers into the weights to make 8-bit weight *and* activation quantization accurate [3], while LLM.int8() decomposes outlier dimensions to enable Int8 matrix multiplication up to 175B parameters without performance loss [30]. Quantization-aware training approaches such as LLM-QAT use data-free distillation to substantially improve low-bit results over PTQ baselines [31].

The central design axis in this literature is how to handle activation outliers. Rotation- and scaling-based methods have become a standard remedy: SmoothRot combines channel-wise scaling with a Hadamard transform and reports reducing 4-bit quantization gaps by 10–30% at no additional latency [32]. A 2025 survey of mixed-precision quantization argues that uniform low-bit schemes (INT8, INT4) can degrade accuracy in sensitive transformer components, motivating selective precision allocation instead [33]. Ultra-low-bit formats are an active frontier: BitNet b1.58 claims ternary 1-bit LLMs can match full-precision quality with new scaling laws and hardware opportunities [34], and MatMul-free language modelling replaces matrix multiplication with lightweight operations while matching state-of-the-art Transformers and reducing memory [35]. Practical kernel work continues alongside the algorithmic work: FireQ pairs INT4-FP8 kernels with RoPE-aware quantization [36], block-based quantization achieves nearly-lossless 6-bit and 4-bit models by reducing numerical scaling offsets [37], and SharQ combines online sparse-dense decomposition with FP4 quantization in a training-free way [38]. A 2026 case study evaluating AWQ, GPTQ, SmoothQuant and FlatQuant on reasoning models across NPU hardware emphasizes significant platform sensitivity — a reminder that PTQ results are hardware-conditional [39].

## KV-cache management and long-context serving

Because decode is bandwidth-bound, the KV cache becomes the second dominant memory consumer and, at long context, often the first. The literature organizes around three strategies: eviction, quantization, and depth/feature merging. H2O identifies "heavy hitter" tokens whose attention scores dominate and evicts the rest, improving throughput and latency [40]; MiniCache merges similar KV states across layers in the depth dimension [41]; DynamicKV adapts retention per layer and per task [42]; and Mustafar applies unstructured per-token magnitude pruning to the KV cache with a custom attention kernel and no fine-tuning [43]. Quantization-based approaches include WKVQuant, which jointly quantizes weights and the KV cache [44], and SAW-INT4, which uses token-wise INT4 quantization with block-diagonal Hadamard rotation for near-lossless serving-grade compression [45]. Structured eviction at page granularity (PagedEviction) and block-sparse prefill attention (FlashPrefill V2) extend this to the serving stack [46][47].

Crucially, this literature is increasingly honest about costs. A comprehensive benchmark of long-context-capable compression approaches asks explicitly what capabilities must be given up, and finds that compression changes long-context behaviour [6]. "The Pitfalls of KV Cache Compression" goes further, showing that compression can degrade multi-instruction task performance and notably cause system-prompt leakage, with improved eviction policies mitigating the effect [7]. For long chain-of-thought reasoning, where KV cache grows during decoding, KARA identifies the limits of threshold-triggered compression policies and proposes sliding-window compression instead [48].

## Speculative decoding and decoding-time acceleration

Speculative decoding is the leading algorithmic acceleration for the decode phase: a cheap drafter proposes several tokens that the target model verifies in parallel, and the scheme is output-preserving and requires no retraining [49]. A comprehensive 2024 survey systematizes the field's draft-then-verify taxonomy [4]. Subsequent work pushes drafting into the model's internal representations: EAGLE performs feature-level speculative sampling losslessly relative to vanilla autoregressive decoding [50], EAGLE-3 replaces feature prediction with direct token prediction plus multi-layer feature fusion to benefit more from training data [51], and Medusa adds multiple parallel decoding heads with tree-based attention [52]. Drafter training has also been connected to knowledge distillation via DistillSpec, which aligns a smaller draft model with a larger target for significant speedups at minimal quality loss [53].

The important counterpoint is measurement. An evaluation of speculative decoding on a production inference engine reports significant gaps between theoretical speedup bounds and actual realized performance across model scales and workloads [21]. Similar realism checks appear in scheduling and routing: production vLLM fleets reportedly provision for worst-case context even though 80–95% of requests are short, wasting 4–8x throughput capacity and causing OOM crashes and preemptions [20].

## Systems, scheduling, and serving efficiency

At the infrastructure level, FlashAttention established the IO-awareness principle: exact attention implemented with tiling to minimize GPU memory reads and writes, arguing that approximate attention methods often fail to produce wall-clock speedups because they ignore memory hierarchy [54]. PagedAttention/vLLM recast serving as a KV-cache memory-management problem, reducing KV waste to raise throughput and becoming the reference implementation for paged caching [5], with alternative approaches such as OS-level demand paging in vAttention [55]. The phase structure of inference then drives scheduling research: SARATHI piggybacks decodes with chunked prefills to fix compute inefficiency [56], Sarathi-Serve generalizes this into stall-free scheduling that tames the throughput-latency tradeoff [17], and DistServe and TetriInfer disaggregate prefill from decode across GPUs to remove inter-phase interference [18][19]. Memory-allocation research continues to adapt as architectures diversify, e.g. Jenga for heterogeneous embedding dimensions and attention patterns [57], and a comparative study benchmarks vLLM against HuggingFace TGI from 7B to 70B [58]. Recent work frames the whole problem as a distributed control plane spanning kernels, quantization, parallelism, networking, autoscaling and SLOs rather than engine-local optimization [59], and SparseX targets segment-level KV-cache sharing because repeated content frequently appears as non-prefix, cross-request or cross-agent segments that a prefix cache cannot exploit [60]. Latency-oriented schedulers (shortest-job-first approximations, prompt-aware scheduling, SLA-aware memory estimation) form a further sub-literature [61][62][63].

Energy and phase asymmetry have become first-class concerns. A systematic study of decode-phase energy across multi-head attention, grouped-query attention, and GQA with sliding-window attention shows attention architecture materially changes energy consumption [64]. Edge-FPGA work states the asymmetry plainly — prefill is compute-bound and dominated by dense matrix-matrix operations, while edge performance degrades as KV-cache bandwidth demands grow with sequence length [23]. This motivates attention-level substitutes: Mamba claims linear-time sequence modelling with inference speed and scalability advantages over Transformers [65], Mamba-3 improves efficiency with a smaller state [66], hybrid transformer+SSM enterprise models report high throughput with minimal reasoning degradation [67], and a quantitative survey scores twenty-one attention methods on an efficiency/expressiveness/interpretability trade-off [68].

## Small language models: definitions, families and scaling

The SLM literature has produced a family of technical reports that has become the field's reference points. phi-3-mini is a 3.8B model reported to be competitive with larger models through heavily filtered and synthetic data plus alignment [69]; Phi-4-Mini and Phi-4-Multimodal use advanced attention and Mixture-of-LoRAs adapters for compact multimodal reasoning [70]; and the 14B Phi-4 reportedly surpasses its teacher model on STEM-focused QA [71]. Gemma 2 improves the Transformer with interleaved local-global attention and grouped-query attention for strong performance relative to size [72], and Gemma 3 adds vision, broader language coverage and longer context [73]. Qwen2.5 improves over Qwen2 through extensive pretraining on roughly 18T tokens plus advanced post-training [74]. TinyLlama is a 1.1B model trained on about 3T tokens using FlashAttention [75], MiniCPM reaches larger-model performance via efficient scaling and LR (WSD) scheduling [76], and PhoneLM is optimized for on-device deployment via architecture search [77].

A distinct architectural claim comes from the sub-billion regime. MobileLLM argues that at this scale architecture — deep-and-thin designs, embedding sharing, grouped-query attention — is more decisive than data or parameter quantity [78], and MobileLLM-R1 revisits the assumption that reasoning requires more than 10T training tokens, showing sub-billion reasoners can emerge with smaller corpora and open recipes [79]. MobileMoE formulates an on-device MoE scaling law with 0.3–0.9B active parameters and identifies an on-device sparsity "sweet spot" [80]. Encoder-decoder architectures have been revisited for parameter efficiency in low-resource SLM settings when combined with distillation and modern embeddings [81], vocabulary scaling laws suggest current models often use suboptimal vocabulary sizes [82], and extreme memory constraints have been targeted directly, e.g. EmbBERT-Q at a 781 kB memory footprint using architectural innovations plus 8-bit quantization [83]. Tiny multimodal models reach similar conclusions: TinyLLaVA finds small-scale models can match larger ones with better data quality and recipes [84].

The scaling-law story is what justifies these models economically. Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models shows that up to roughly 4 epochs of repeated data are about as useful as new data, after which returns diminish toward zero [85]. Against that constraint, data *quality* substitutes for quantity: phi-1 attains high coding accuracy with far fewer parameters using "textbook-quality" curated data [86], TinyStories shows synthetic simple stories let very small models produce fluent, coherent text [87], and TinyGSM drives a 1.3B model above 80% on GSM8k with synthetic math data [88]. Data-centric pretraining then goes long: SmolLM2 is a 1.7B model overtrained on approximately 11T tokens across a multi-stage mixture of web, math, code and instruction data [13]. Beyond Chinchilla-Optimal formalizes the economics — incorporating inference cost into scaling laws implies models facing large inference demand should be smaller and trained longer than compute-optimal [14]; related work derives inference-aware scaling laws with architecture search [15] and prescriptive laws under data constraints [16], while other studies attribute discrepancies among compute-optimal laws to accounting, warmup and optimizer tuning [89] and argue performance depends primarily on total compute rather than the size/data split [90].

Capability comparisons are nuanced rather than uniform. SLMs can beat larger models on narrow, fine-tuned application tasks — a supply-chain case study reports better accuracy and running time, especially with small fine-tuning datasets [91] — and SLM judges in the 0.6B–14B range have been benchmarked as competitive across many judgment tasks [92]. But ultra-compact models still lag medium-scale SLMs on agentic edge tasks [93] and on-device user profiling [94], and multiple surveys converge on the need for standardization of SLM definitions, acquisition, enhancement and trustworthiness [12][26][27].

## Adaptation, distillation, and deployment practice

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) is the standard route to specialize small models. LoRA reduces trainable parameters and GPU memory while matching or improving downstream performance [95]; QA-LoRA quantizes weights during fine-tuning and integrates auxiliary weights post-training to cut adaptation cost [96]; L4Q performs LoRA-wise quantization-aware training to reach sub-4-bit precision with accuracy comparable to quantized PEFT [97]; and sparse-matrix tuning narrows the PEFT-to-full-fine-tuning gap while cutting GPU memory by up to 67% [98]. QDyLoRA addresses the rigidity of fixed LoRA ranks in QLoRA-style pipelines [99]. Energy is increasingly counted alongside quality: a comparison of full fine-tuning, LoRA, LoRA+, QLoRA and BitFit on four on-device SLMs accounts for energy consumption as well as accuracy, motivated by VRAM, time and energy costs of personalization [100].

Knowledge distillation is the main mechanism for *creating* small models. Surveys cover LLM knowledge distillation broadly and distinguish white-box from black-box methods [101][102], with recent work unifying on-policy distillation through an f-divergence framework [103]. Distillation is also fused with the efficiency stack: DistillSpec aligns drafters for speculative decoding [53], and curriculum-based supervised fine-tuning combined with offline on-policy distillation yields strong edge-deployable SLM performance with good generalization [104][75]. On the deployment side, PTQ to 4-bit and the GGUF format are reported to enable mobile deployment while reducing model size [105], a unified empirical evaluation compares llama.cpp quantization options on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct across bit depths to inform practical choices [106], and mobile cold-start latency has been attacked with NPU-aware quantization, SIMD-friendly packing and dynamic CPU-NPU coordination [107]. Phase-aware workload characterization across edge SoC generations and backends provides the measurement substrate for such work [108], while FPGA accelerators using table-lookup (LUT) computation claim lower latency and higher energy efficiency than GPUs [109], and vector table lookup (Vec-LUT) work notes that CPU LUT inference can outperform NPUs while parallel inference underutilizes memory bandwidth [110].

## Evaluation, benchmarking, and known failure modes

Evaluation of small and compressed models is where the efficiency literature is weakest and most contested. Benchmark data contamination undermines the numbers themselves: string-matching decontamination is insufficient, and LLM-based rephrasing detection reveals significant test overlap [111]; a dedicated survey frames benchmark data contamination as an integrity problem [112]; and one measurement study shows contamination can significantly impact scores in a model- and scale-dependent way [113]. Contamination-resistant alternatives have been proposed, including MMLU-CF, which avoids both unintentional and malicious leakage [114], LiveBench, an auto-scored, bias-resilient benchmark [115], AntiLeak-Bench, which constructs samples from knowledge absent from training sets [116], and Caesar-cipher-based contamination-resistance benchmarks [117]. Individual benchmarks have also been audited: "Are We Done with MMLU?" identifies numerous errors and produces MMLU-Redux, showing the errors distort model rankings [118], while MMLU-Pro adds harder reasoning items and removes trivial ones for better stability and discrimination [119], and MMLU-Pro+ probes shortcut learning [120]. Beyond single-number accuracy, a multi-variant reliability audit finds that prompt robustness does not correlate with model size and that confidence signals are fragile [121], and a "tokenomics" study of SLM ensembles on a 541-prompt IFEval evaluation reports 97.34% strict accuracy, beating a frontier model by 5.81 points in a lower-cost regime, arguing that evaluation must extend to cost, latency and token efficiency [122].

The failure modes are systematic. Safety benchmarks appear unreliable for small models specifically: an evaluation of automated safety benchmarks finds ambiguous judgments for SLMs due to a capability-safety confound, producing brittle rankings [123]. Quantization can silently damage trustworthiness — moderate bit-widths can even improve some ethical dimensions while extreme quantization degrades trustworthiness [8], quantized checkpoints that pass quality metrics may still pose safety risks [9], post-training quantization produces bias emergence undetected by standard metrics [10], GGUF quantization is vulnerable to adversarial interference [124], and low-rank compression can pass every data-free quality guard yet induce agentic procedure errors [125]. Benchmarking pre-trained versus compressed SLMs suggests compression of reliable models is preferable to training small models from scratch, with distillation further improving reliability [126]. Reasoning distillation into small models has known bottlenecks: chain-of-thought distillation helps students below 100B parameters [127], and decomposition-based distillation transfers step-by-step reasoning [128], but distilled long chain-of-thought data poses learning difficulty and inherits over-thinking bias in small models [129], and distilled behaviour may not generalize to novel test-time contexts [130]. Test-time compute is likewise not a free win: self-refinement on small reasoning models largely consolidates probability mass onto already-reachable solutions, exposing an execution bottleneck [131], and at equal candidate budget the choice of batched versus sequential generation changes both energy and performance on Phi-3-mini and Qwen2.5-1.5B [132]. Evaluation of long context remains problematic as well, since perplexity ignores critical tokens [133] and models use long contexts unevenly ("lost in the middle") [134] with brittleness at longer lengths and sensitive prompt positioning [135]. Efforts to make evaluation trustworthy include shortcut-neuron analysis to suppress contaminated circuits [136].

Energy and cost measurement is emerging as a parallel evaluation axis. On-device battery studies across 18 models document the environmental cost of local inference [137], prompting proposals to evaluate inference as "energy-to-token production" with metrics beyond accuracy and latency [138], while profiling frameworks such as MELODI record inference energy consumption [139].

## Trends and open problems

Several trends are clear over the last two years. First, the centre of gravity has shifted from single-model compression toward *system-level* co-design: scheduling [17][56][18][61], memory allocation [57], cache sharing [60], routing and cascading across models [29], and distributed control planes [59] now deliver much of the reported efficiency, and model routing/cascading is emerging as its own sub-field [29]. Second, efficiency research is becoming more sceptical of its own claims: theoretical speculative-decoding bounds overstate production speedups [21], KV compression has documented behavioural pitfalls such as system-prompt leakage [7], and fleets waste capacity through configuration-traffic mismatch [20]. Third, the small-model literature has moved from "can it work?" to "what recipe?" — data quality [86][87][88], extreme overtraining [13], architecture at fixed scale [78][80], and vocabulary design [82] are all active levers.

Open problems remain substantial. There is still no agreed definition of a "small" language model, which makes cross-paper comparison difficult [11][12][27][26]. Efficiency and safety are not aligned: quantized models can pass quality checks while failing safety behaviour [9][10][124][125], and standard safety benchmarks are unreliable for SLMs [123], so the field lacks evaluation protocols that jointly measure efficiency and trustworthiness. Reasoning in small models is unresolved — distillation inherits teacher biases [129] and may not generalize [130], and test-time compute can fail to produce genuinely new solutions [131]. Long-context efficiency is a specific weak point, since compression degrades multi-instruction behaviour [7] and long-context metrics are themselves flawed [133][134][135]. Finally, energy reporting is inconsistent: profiling frameworks exist [139] and position papers call for energy-aware evaluation [138], but most papers still report only latency and throughput, and results are hardware-conditional enough that PTQ comparisons can reverse across platforms [39].
