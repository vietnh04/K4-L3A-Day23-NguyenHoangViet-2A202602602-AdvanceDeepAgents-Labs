# Video and Multimodal Generation: A Literature Survey

## TL;DR

- Video generation has converged on latent diffusion with transformer (DiT) backbones: the field moved from pixel-space U-Nets with temporal attention [1] to latent video diffusion [2][3][4], then to cascaded high-resolution pipelines [4][5][6][7], and finally to large DiT-based latent video models such as CogVideoX, Open-Sora and Step-Video-T2V [8][9][10][11].
- A parallel, non-diffusion lineage tokenises video and models it autoregressively with language-model machinery; MAGVIT, VideoPoet and the MAGVIT-v2 "tokenizer is key" result argue that a strong tokenizer lets an LLM beat diffusion [12][13][14], while LARP improves the tokenizer itself with learned queries [15].
- Unified multimodal models are split between fully token-based early-fusion systems (Chameleon, Emu3, AnyGPT, Unified-IO 2) [16][17][18][19] and hybrids that pair autoregressive text with diffusion or flow over continuous visual latents (Transfusion, Show-o, JanusFlow, LatentLM) [20][21][22][23]; a 2025 survey organises the whole space into diffusion-, autoregressive- and hybrid-based families [24].
- Evaluation has become a discipline of its own: VBench decomposes video quality into hierarchical, disentangled dimensions [25], VBench++/VBench-2.0 extend it to more tasks and to "intrinsic faithfulness" [26][27], and a series of papers document that holistic metrics such as FID and CLIPScore are inadequate, motivating reward models like VisionReward and VideoScore2 [28][29][30][31][32][33].
- The most active applied frontier in 2025-2026 is efficiency and interactivity: consistency/distribution-matching distillation [34][35][36][37][38][39], few-step streaming autoregression [40][41][42][43][44], KV-cache compression [45][46][47], and real-time interactive world models [48][49][50].

## Background

**What is being generated.** Video generation is the task of synthesising a sequence of frames, typically conditioned on text, an image, or a control signal. Multimodal generation generalises this to producing and consuming several modalities — text, image, video, audio and action — within one system. The two research threads share their core machinery: a generative model over a (usually compressed) representation, a way to condition it, and a way to evaluate the output.

**Foundations: diffusion and latent compression.** Most current systems inherit the denoising diffusion framework, in which a network learns to invert a noising process. The foundational video extension, "Video Diffusion Models", kept an image U-Net but added temporal (3D) convolution and time-attention layers, trained jointly on images and video, and used conditional sampling to extend videos over time [1]. Because pixel-space video is expensive, the field quickly moved to latent diffusion: MagicVideo adapted a latent U-Net to text-to-video [2] and LVDM proposed a low-dimensional 3D latent space with hierarchical diffusion for long video [3]. The "factorise space and time" motif — spatial layers within a frame plus temporal layers across frames — established here remains the dominant architectural pattern.

**The canonical recipe.** "Align your Latents" / VideoLDM is the most influential concrete recipe: start from a pretrained image latent diffusion model, insert temporal attention into its down- and up-sampling blocks, finetune on video, then add a temporal decoder and upsampler for higher frame rates and resolution, enabling videos up to 1280x2048 [4]. LaVie chains a base video LDM with temporal interpolation and video super-resolution models [5], W.A.L.T. applies the same cascade with transformer backbones, causal encoding and window attention [6], and Stable Video Diffusion scaled latent video diffusion with systematic data curation and staged image-to-video finetuning [7]. Two surveys corroborate the overall trajectory and the shift toward hybrid diffusion-transformer designs [51][52].

**The transformer takeover.** The DiT paper showed that replacing the U-Net with a transformer over patchified latents both improves image quality and scales predictably with compute [53]. Latte ported this directly to video using spatio-temporal tokens and factorised spatial/temporal attention blocks [54], and VDT applied a video diffusion transformer with masked modelling to general-purpose video tasks [55]. The corresponding survey literature treats this as a phase change from GAN/VAE to latent diffusion to DiT backbones [51][52].

## Theme 1: Architectures and Backbones for Video Generation

**Latent video diffusion and cascades.** The early consensus was that compressing video into a low-dimensional latent space is necessary for tractability [2][3], and that high resolution is best reached by cascading a low-resolution video model with temporal interpolation and super-resolution stages [4][5]. W.A.L.T. demonstrated the same cascade with transformer backbones, causal encoding and window attention, reaching photorealistic quality [6], while Stable Video Diffusion showed that data curation and staged finetuning are as important as architecture at scale [7].

**DiT as the default backbone.** CogVideoX pairs a 3D causal VAE with an "expert" transformer and 3D full attention [8]; Open-Sora uses a Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformer with a 3D autoencoder [9]; Open-Sora Plan substitutes a Wavelet-Flow VAE and a Joint Image-Video Skiparse Denoiser [10]; and Step-Video-T2V is a 30B-parameter DiT with 3D full attention and a deep-compression VAE [11]. The consistent pattern across these systems is "large DiT plus strong video VAE", with the transformer providing scalability and the VAE controlling the token budget.

**The token/autoregressive alternative.** A separate lineage tokenises video into a discrete codebook and models it autoregressively. MAGVIT introduced a 3D video tokenizer feeding a masked generative transformer [12]; VideoPoet frames video, image and audio generation as next-token prediction by a single decoder-only LLM over discrete tokens [13]. MAGVIT-v2 makes the strongest claim of this line — that with a sufficiently good video tokenizer using a lookup-free quantizer, a language model outperforms diffusion for image and video generation [14]. LARP improves the tokenizer side by replacing uniform patch tokenization with learned queries and a lightweight autoregressive prior [15].

**Video tokenizers and 3D VAEs as a first-class design axis.** Because both diffusion and autoregressive approaches operate on compressed representations, the video VAE/tokenizer directly sets the quality-cost trade-off. CV-VAE trains a video VAE compatible with existing image VAEs to allow spatio-temporal compression while reusing image priors [56]; CogVideoX's 3D causal VAE [8] and Step-Video-T2V's deep-compression VAE [11] are large-scale instances of the same principle. The compression rate — tokens per video — trades reconstruction fidelity against the cost of the downstream generator.

**Attention design and the efficiency pressure.** Full 3D attention captures all spatio-temporal interactions but scales quadratically, so factorised spatial-plus-temporal attention became the standard compromise [4][54][55]. Recent work refines this specifically for efficiency: RainFusion is a training-free sparse-attention method exploiting multi-dimensional visual redundancy in 3D-attention video models [57], and MoGA uses mixture-of-groups attention to make long-video generation tractable [58]. This is the same tension that reappears at the systems level in Theme 4.

## Theme 2: Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation

**The unification problem.** The central question of this thread is whether one network can both *understand* (image/video-to-text, VQA) and *generate* (text-to-image/video/audio). The survey "Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation Models" organises approaches into diffusion-based, autoregressive-based and hybrid families, and identifies the core tension: understanding needs semantically abstract features while generation needs pixel-faithful reconstruction, pulling the shared visual representation in opposite directions [24].

**Token-based (fully discrete) unification.** Chameleon is the canonical mixed-modal early-fusion model: images and text are converted into a single interleaved token sequence and one transformer models any mixture, with training-stability techniques needed to make early fusion work [16]. Emu3 pushes the "next-token prediction is all you need" thesis, using a discrete visual tokenizer and outperforming diffusion and compositional baselines on both generation and perception [17]; Emu3.5 scales this to a native multimodal "world learner" that predicts next states in vision and language, adding reinforcement learning and a Discrete Diffusion Adaptation for efficient inference [59]. AnyGPT applies discrete sequence modelling across text, image, speech and music [18], MIO builds a token-based foundation model with causal multimodal modelling [60], and Unified-IO 2 unifies vision, language, audio and *action* in a single autoregressive transformer over a shared token space [19].

**Hybrid diffusion + LLM unification.** Transfusion trains one transformer with two objectives — next-token prediction on text and diffusion on image latents — and reports that this matches specialised models while scaling across combined text and image data [20]. LatentLM generalises the idea with "next-token diffusion" over continuous VAE latents, covering image generation and speech synthesis in a causal transformer [23]. Show-o mixes autoregressive text modelling with discrete diffusion for images in one transformer [21]; Show-o Turbo accelerates unified denoising via consistency distillation and trajectory segmentation without hurting quality [61]; Show-o2 adds a 3D causal VAE and flow matching for native image and video unification [62]. JanusFlow instead harmonises autoregression with rectified flow inside the language model [22].

**Decoupled and dual-encoder designs.** Janus explicitly decouples visual encoding — one pathway for understanding (semantic features) and another for generation (discrete tokens) — inside a single transformer, to stop the two tasks from fighting over one representation [63]; Janus-Pro improves this with optimised training, expanded data and larger models, boosting both understanding and text-to-image generation [64]. Architecture-level conflict mitigation is an active sub-field: UniFork explores Y-shaped modality alignment [65], Uni-X uses a two-end-separated X-shape to reduce gradient conflict [66], HBridge bridges heterogeneous experts in an H-shape [67], and state-space approaches OmniMamba [68] and Mixture-of-Mamba [69] use decoupled vocabularies and modality-aware sparsity for efficient unification.

**Unified tokenizers as the bottleneck.** Because tokenizers designed for generation lose semantics and vice versa, a wave of work targets a shared visual token space. UniTok uses multi-codebook quantization for competitive generation and understanding [70]; TokenFlow uses a dual codebook with shared indices to decouple semantic and pixel-level features [71]; VQRAE combines continuous semantic features with discrete tokens for understanding, generation and reconstruction [72]; AToken is a transformer-based tokenizer spanning images, video and 3D [73]; Ming-UniVision uses a unified *continuous* latent tokenizer in an autoregressive framework [74]; and UniCode^2 proposes cascaded large-scale codebooks feeding diffusion decoders [75].

**Any-to-any and omni models.** Qwen3-Omni targets state-of-the-art results across text, image, audio and video using a Thinker-Talker MoE architecture with a lightweight causal ConvNet for streaming synthesis [76]. NExT-OMNI is an open-source any-to-any omnimodal foundation model based on discrete flow matching [77], and Omni-Diffusion is presented as the first any-to-any multimodal language model based on mask-based discrete diffusion, unifying text, speech and image [78]. BAGEL is an open-source unified pretraining model reported to outperform existing models across both generation and understanding [79], and MODUS is a decoder-only model that symmetrically handles arbitrary inputs and outputs for chained generation and cross-modal self-verification [80]. Earlier alignment-style systems such as AnyMAL handled text, image, video, audio and IMU inputs but generated text only [81].

**Instruction-following and controllability.** DreamOmni2 defines multimodal instruction-based editing and generation using feature mixing, index encoding and joint training with a vision-language model [82]; ShareGPT-4o-Image / Janus-4o target GPT-4o-level instruction-aligned image generation via a large dataset and multimodal model [83]; MGIE derives expressive edit instructions from an MLLM to improve instruction-based editing [84]; and In-Video-Instructions shows video generative models can interpret visual instructions embedded inside frames as generative control for image-to-video [85]. UniRL and reinforcement-learning approaches raise comprehension and generation quality jointly without external data [86][87]. On evaluation, MME-Unify benchmarks unified models on traditional and mixed-modality tasks and finds that current models still fall short of expectations on unified capabilities [88].

## Theme 3: Datasets, Benchmarks and Evaluation

**Video-generation benchmarks.** VBench is the central suite for video generative models, decomposing "video generation quality" into hierarchical, disentangled dimensions, each with tailored prompts and evaluation methods, and reporting alignment with human perception while revealing gaps versus image generation models [25]. VBench++ extends the suite with fine-grained metrics and human preference annotations and covers additional tasks such as image-to-video [26]. VBench-2.0 reframes evaluation around "intrinsic faithfulness" — human fidelity, controllability, creativity, physics and commonsense [27]. EvalCrafter benchmarks large video generation models across visual quality, content, motion and textual quality, reporting alignment of its metrics with user opinions [89], and FETV adds a multi-aspect, temporal-aware categorisation of text prompts for fine-grained evaluation [90].

**Critiques of existing benchmarks.** DynamicEval explicitly criticises VBench and EvalCrafter for emphasising subject-centric, static-camera prompts and for aggregating video-level scores into a single model-level ranking; it adds dynamic camera-motion evaluation and argues that video-level evaluation matters for choosing the better video for a given prompt [91].

**Image-generation benchmarks.** GenEval is an object-focused framework for compositional text-to-image alignment, motivated by the observation that FID and CLIPScore provide only holistic measures and are unsuited to fine-grained, instance-level analysis [28]. T2I-CompBench targets open-world compositional text-to-image generation with dedicated metrics [92].

**Datasets and curation pipelines.** Panda-70M is a ~70M video-text pair dataset curated and annotated using multiple cross-modality captioning teachers, reported to improve video captioning, retrieval and generation [93]. Koala-36M targets consistency between fine-grained conditions and video content via processing and filtering that improves temporal consistency, text-video alignment and quality [94]. VidGen-1M curates high-quality videos with detailed captions and temporal consistency [95], ViMix-14M is a curated multi-source dataset of 14M video-text pairs with long-form captions and crawl-free access [96], and Data Filtering Networks select high-relevance samples to build better training sets [97]. A controlled study of training data concludes that balanced content distribution and high-quality captions are essential for generalisation and efficiency, and that poor pre-training data is hard to recover from [98].

**Human preference and reward models.** HPSv2 provides a dataset and scoring model that better capture human preferences for text-to-image synthesis [99], and HPSv3 moves toward a "wide-spectrum" human preference score trained with a wide-spectrum dataset and an uncertainty-aware ranking loss [100]. VisionReward learns fine-grained multi-dimensional human preferences for both image and video generation, explicitly criticising prior reward models as black-box, uninterpretable and prone to unexpected biases [29]. VideoScore2 is a multi-dimensional, interpretable evaluation framework for text-to-video that assesses visual quality, alignment and consistency with supporting rationales [30].

**The metric-versus-human-judgment gap.** T2VScore is proposed because existing automated metrics are inadequate, combining text-video alignment and video quality [31]. ETVA evaluates text-to-video alignment via fine-grained question generation and answering and reports significantly outperforming CLIPScore [32]. cFreD, a Conditional Fréchet Distance metric for both text-to-image and text-to-video, is introduced to close the gap between visual quality and semantic alignment and is reported to correlate better with human judgments than existing methods [33]. Taken together, these results support a clear survey conclusion: holistic metrics are insufficient, reward models are increasingly used for alignment, and prompt sensitivity plus video-level versus model-level aggregation remain open methodological problems [28][31][91].

## Theme 4: Controllability, World Models and Efficiency

**Controllable generation.** ControlNet-style conditioning for video originated with Control-A-Video, which injects control signals through spatial-temporal self-attention plus residual-based noise initialisation to improve temporal consistency [101]. Motion-I2V addresses image-to-video via explicit motion modelling and trajectory prediction for consistency and controllability [102]. Camera control then branched into explicit adapters and geometry: I2VControl-Camera uses an independent adapter to control camera motion while allowing adjustable subject-motion strength [103]; VD3D conditions large video diffusion transformers on a 3D camera trajectory through spatiotemporal embeddings [104]; and MotionMaster offers training-free camera motion transfer by decoupling camera and object motion [105]. Newer work moves toward semantic and 3D-editable control: SemCam replaces explicit trajectories with a reference video plus a target motion label such as keeping a frontal view of a moving subject [106], the Generative Cinematographer lifts a single image into an editable 3D scene scaffold where camera paths and 3D motion are authored jointly [107], and TKCAM synthesises camera trajectories from text and keyframes using a discretised kinematic feature with a two-stage masked transformer [108].

**Unified control and editing frameworks.** EditStream unifies text-to-video, image-to-video, video-to-video, editing propagation, reference-guided editing and camera-pose change in a single DiT through task-specific conditioning, then converts it to a few-step autoregressive streaming model [109]. IC-Effect performs few-shot video effects editing via in-context learning and sparse tokenization to preserve spatial and temporal consistency [110]. Subject and identity consistency is a parallel theme: Magic-Me enables identity-controllable video generation [111], ID-Composer uses hierarchical identity-preserving attention plus a VLM for multi-subject synthesis [112], and Memento reconstructs recurring subjects with a dual-query memory mechanism for long-form consistency [113].

**World models and interactive environments.** Matrix-Game 2.0 is an open-source real-time streaming interactive world model using few-step autoregressive diffusion [49]; Matrix-Game 3.0 adds memory-augmented diffusion for real-time 720p synthesis with long-horizon temporal consistency [48]; and Matrix-Game 3.5 adds geometry-aware patch memory, static-dynamic disentanglement and progressive distillation [50]. The broader field is consolidating around "world models as game engines": one paper reframes the problem as action-state-observation loops [114], and a survey covers action-conditioned generation, long-horizon memory and real-time responsiveness with benchmarks across games, driving and robotics [115]. Multi-agent simulation is emerging with Gamma-World's agent encoding and sparse hub attention [116] and Artemis's geometry-grounded shared 3D state for multi-agent driving [117]. Driving world models are represented by DriVerse, which uses trajectory prompting and motion alignment [118], and NVIDIA OmniDreams, a real-time action-conditioned closed-loop autonomous-driving simulation built from a Cosmos diffusion foundation model [119], with DrivingGen as a comprehensive benchmark measuring visual realism, trajectory plausibility, temporal coherence and controllability [120]. WorldGuide formulates procedural video generation as closed-loop goal-directed tasks [121].

**Efficiency: distillation and few-step streaming.** Three thrusts stand out. First, consistency models: VideoLCM adapts latent consistency for few-step video [34], AnimateLCM adds decoupled consistency learning compatible with personalisation adapters [35], DCM uses a dual-expert consistency model [36], and score-regularised continuous-time consistency brings this to large-scale distillation [37], while the Motion Consistency Model disentangles motion and appearance [38] and OSV targets one-step image-to-video [122]. Second, distribution-matching distillation: CoDMD diagnoses DMD's layout instability, oversaturation and broken dynamics under limited function evaluations as an intra-sample limitation and adds copula-aware relational constraints [39]; Radian complements on-policy distillation with representation-space adversarial distillation [123]; Stream-R1 reweights supervision by reliability and perplexity [124]; and Adaptive Video Distillation mitigates oversaturation and temporal collapse [125]. Third, autoregressive and few-step streaming: Causal Forcing++ distills bidirectional teachers into frame-wise autoregressive students at one to two sampling steps [40][126], UnStep shows a training-free wrapper can run few-step causal models with fewer DiT steps than distillation plus a bounded KV window [41], and Rolling Forcing [42], Deep Forcing [127], LongLive [44] and autoregressive adversarial post-training [128] target real-time long video.

**Efficiency: KV cache and attention.** KV-cache management has become a first-class research thrust. Forcing-KV splits heads into static and dynamic groups for hybrid compression [46], Pyramid Forcing applies a head-aware pyramidal KV cache policy [45], Quant VideoGen introduces 2-bit KV-cache quantization for autoregressive long video [47], and FlowCache uses chunkwise caching for autoregressive video generation [43]. Surveys of scalable video world models frame efficiency as the key gating factor for practical interactive deployment [129].

**Avatars and talking heads.** FantasyTalking uses a pretrained video diffusion transformer with motion-dynamics control for realistic talking portraits [130]. ChatAnyone is a stylised real-time portrait generator using hierarchical motion diffusion plus explicit hand control [131], and Hallo-Live achieves real-time streaming joint audio-video avatar generation with asynchronous dual-stream diffusion, Future-Expanding Attention and human-centric preference-guided distillation [132]. Knot Forcing targets real-time infinite interactive portrait animation [133], while 3D and Gaussian approaches include SuperHead, which uses 3D generative priors to enhance low-quality talking heads [134], and SyncTalk++, which uses Gaussian splatting for synchronised identity, lip, expression and pose [135]. An earlier line used implicit facial keypoint editing for controllable talking-face generation [136], and audio generation is being folded into the same world-model stack [137].

**Safety, watermarking and provenance.** In-generation watermarking is a leading defence: Safe-Sora embeds invisible watermarks during text-to-video synthesis using hierarchical adaptive matching and a 3D-wavelet-enhanced Mamba architecture [138], and Video Signature integrates watermarks directly into latent video diffusion generation [139]. Detection-side work includes VidGuard-R1, which uses a reasoning MLLM with reinforcement learning for AI-generated video detection and explanation [140], and the AEGIS authenticity benchmark for AI-generated video sequences [141]. Evidence favours watermarks over passive detection: a benchmark comparing both found watermark-based detectors more effective and robust [142], while a study of detector robustness documents fundamental vulnerabilities to diffusion purification and model-substitution attacks [143].

## Trends and Open Problems

**Recent advances (last two years).** The clearest architectural trend is the consolidation of DiT-based latent video diffusion as the default, instantiated at very large scale by CogVideoX [8], Open-Sora [9], Open-Sora Plan [10] and Step-Video-T2V [11], with the video VAE treated as a first-class design component [56][8][11]. In parallel, the autoregressive token line has matured from MAGVIT and VideoPoet [12][13] through the "tokenizer is key" argument [14] to better tokenizers such as LARP [15]. Unification of understanding and generation has moved from a research curiosity to a mainstream agenda [24], with a clear split between fully token-based early fusion [16][17][18][19] and hybrids that mix autoregressive text with diffusion or flow [20][21][22][23], and with unified tokenizers identified as the shared bottleneck [70][71][72][73][74][75].

**Systems have become interactive and real-time.** The most striking shift of 2025-2026 is from offline clip generation to streaming, interactive generation. Few-step autoregressive distillation [40][126][41], KV-cache compression [45][46][47] and caching [43] now make real-time long video plausible, and the world-model literature has reframed generative video as an action-conditioned game-engine loop [114][115] with concrete systems for interactive gameplay [49][48][50], autonomous driving [118][119] and multi-agent simulation [116][117]. Benchmarking for these settings is emerging in tandem [120][115][91].

**Unsolved challenges and open debates.** Several debates remain unresolved. First, *diffusion versus autoregressive generation*: the token-based camp claims that a good tokenizer lets language models beat diffusion [14], while DiT-based diffusion remains the dominant industrial choice [8][9][10][11]; hybrid designs such as Transfusion and Show-o explicitly attempt to have both [20][21]. Second, *unification versus specialisation*: dual-encoder designs such as Janus exist precisely because sharing one representation harms one task [63][64], and benchmarks show unified models still fall short of expectations [88]; whether a single shared tokenizer can serve understanding and generation remains open [70][71][72]. Third, *evaluation*: holistic metrics like FID and CLIPScore are widely acknowledged as inadequate [28][31], benchmarks are criticised for static-camera subject-centric bias and flawed aggregation [91], and reward models are proposed as a fix but are themselves criticised as black-box and biased [29][30]. Fourth, *data*: scale has been demonstrated [93][94][95][96], but a controlled study argues that distribution and caption quality matter more than raw scale and that bad pre-training data is hard to recover from [98]. Fifth, *provenance*: watermarks outperform passive detectors in benchmarks [142] yet detectors remain vulnerable to purification and model-substitution attacks [143], so robust attribution for generated video is unresolved.
