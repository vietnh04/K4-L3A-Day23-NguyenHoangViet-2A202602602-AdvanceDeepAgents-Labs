# World Models: A Literature Survey of Architectures, Evaluation, and Applications

## TL;DR

- A "world model" is an unsupervised generative network that learns a compressed spatial and temporal representation of an environment, letting an agent train inside its own "dream" and transfer the resulting policy back to reality [1][2]; modern surveys reframe these as *internal simulators* that learn the structure and dynamics of an environment to enable prediction, planning and reasoning [3][4].
- Architectures have diversified into three dominant branches: recurrent/latent state-space models (the RSSM backbone of the Dreamer family) [5][6][7], token-autoregressive transformers (IRIS, GAIA-1, Genie) [8][9][10], and generative diffusion video world models (Sora, DIAMOND, GameNGen, Oasis, UniSim) [11][12][13][14][15]; a fourth line, JEPA, predicts in representation space rather than pixels [16][17].
- Model-based agents deliver large, consistent sample-efficiency gains — EfficientZero reaches 194.3% mean human-normalized score on Atari 100k [18] and Simulus/TWISTER push to 162% mean [19] — but generative-video benchmarks rest on perceptual metrics (FVD, PSNR, LPIPS) that are content-biased and weakly correlated with control utility [20][21][22].
- Evaluation research converges on a *perception–functionality gap*: visual realism does not predict controllability, memory, or embodied utility [23][24][25], and Physics-IQ shows realism is statistically uncorrelated with physical competence [26][27].
- Long-horizon consistency, compounding drift, and objective mismatch remain the core unsolved problems; recent work shows failure can originate in the *planner's cost function* rather than the predictor [28][29], and that exploitation/"exploitability gap" diagnostics are almost never reported [22].

## Background

In the modern deep-learning sense, a world model is a generative neural network trained without reward supervision to learn a compressed spatial and temporal representation of a reinforcement-learning environment; its features feed a compact controller, and — crucially — the controller can be trained entirely inside the model's own "dream" (a hallucinated rollout) and transferred back to the real environment [1]. The original interactive article motivates this cognitively: "Can agents learn inside of their own dreams?", grounding the idea in Jay Wright Forrester's notion of mental models and in neuroscience evidence that perception is governed by the brain's predictive internal model conditioned on motor actions [2]. That inaugural agent factored into a large vision encoder, a temporal memory (MDN-RNN) predictor, and a tiny controller, most of it trained without reward [2].

Contemporary surveys generalize the definition. One comprehensive survey defines world models as "internal simulators that learn the structure and dynamics of an environment", enabling agents to predict, plan and reason within learned representations, and proposes a multi-axis taxonomy spanning architectures, training methods, reasoning paradigms and applications [3]. A survey for embodied AI formalizes the setting along three axes — functionality (decision-coupled vs. general-purpose), temporal modeling (sequential simulation/inference vs. global difference prediction), and spatial representation (global latent vector, token feature sequence, spatial latent grid, decomposed rendering) [4]. A parallel 3D/4D survey observes that the absence of a standardized definition has produced "fragmented and sometimes inconsistent claims" [30].

The organizing theoretical frame is the model-based versus model-free distinction in reinforcement learning: model-based methods learn or use a dynamics model to compute or plan values, while model-free methods cache values directly [3]. World models are the parametric realization of the "model", enabling forward and counterfactual rollouts for planning or policy learning. Two classic precursors are central. MuZero combines tree search (MCTS) with a learned latent model to reach superhuman play in Atari, Go, Chess and Shogi without access to the environment's true dynamics, demonstrating that a learned latent model suffices for strong planning [31]. The successor representation (Dayan 1993) caches a "predictive map" of expected future state occupancies, sitting between model-free efficiency and model-based flexibility by supporting flexible revaluation under reward change and showing a signature of insensitivity to transition changes but flexibility to reward changes [32][33].

## Foundations and Early World Models

The single most influential architectural descendant of the original proposal is the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), introduced with PlaNet and reused across the Dreamer family. The RSSM splits the latent state into a deterministic recurrent "belief" h_t = f(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1}) and a stochastic categorical latent z_t, trained with a prior p(z_t|h_t) used during imagination (no observation) and a posterior q(z_t|h_t, o_t) used during encoding [34]. DreamerV1 showed that behaviors can be learned "purely by latent imagination", backpropagating analytic gradients of learned state values through trajectories imagined in the compact latent space and exceeding prior methods in data-efficiency, computation time, and final performance on 20 visual control tasks [5].

The line then scales in two directions. DreamerV2 was reported as the first world-model RL agent to reach human-level performance on the Atari benchmark, learning purely within the latent space of a world model trained from pixels, and introduced categorical (discrete) latents plus KL balancing [34]. DreamerV3 generalized the algorithm to over 150 diverse tasks with a single configuration and was the first to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula, using robustness techniques such as normalization, balancing and transformations [6]; the Nature version details block-GRU sequence models with RMSNorm/SiLU, symlog observation transforms, symexp two-hot reward/critic losses, percentile return normalization and adaptive gradient clipping [7].

A second branch replaces recurrent latent filtering with sequence attention. Empirical comparison of RNN, Transformer and state-space (S4) backbones in model-based RL found that an S4-based world model (S4WM) with latent variable modeling claims superior long-term memory and efficiency relative to RNN and Transformer backbones [35]. Scaling transformers to extremely long context — a "world model on million-length video and language" using masked-sequence packing and RingAttention — bridges autoregressive video and language world models [36]. Together these define the transformer/state-space branch as the scaling-oriented alternative to the probabilistic RSSM.

## Architectures and Training Objectives

Modern world models cluster into three dominant architectural families, plus a representation-space alternative. Within the latent state-space family, DreamerV3's RSSM couples a convolutional encoder, a block-GRU sequence model, stochastic categorical latents, and reward/continue/decoder heads, trained with a reconstruction-based prediction loss plus dynamics and representation KL losses regularized by free bits (1 nat) and KL balancing, with weights roughly 1/1/0.1 [6]. Its defining property is configuration robustness: one fixed hyperparameter set solves over 150 tasks and larger models both score higher and need fewer environment interactions [6][7]. TD-MPC2 belongs to this family conceptually but discards the decoder: it learns an implicit, decoder-free latent dynamics model with encoder, latent dynamics, reward, terminal-value and policy-prior components, trained with joint-embedding prediction + reward prediction + TD-learning, and controls via latent-space MPPI planning, scaling to a single 317M-parameter agent across 80 tasks, with capabilities shown to increase with model and data size [37].

The second family reframes the problem as token sequence modeling. IRIS pairs a discrete (VQ-style) autoencoder with an autoregressive transformer over latent tokens and learns its policy by imagination, reaching a mean human-normalized score of 1.046 on Atari 100k with roughly two hours of gameplay, beating humans on 10 of 26 games [8]. GAIA-1 applies the same recipe to driving — VQ tokens plus an autoregressive transformer for next-token prediction, with a separate video diffusion decoder for pixels and multimodal text/action conditioning — though it is not real-time [10]. Genie scales this into a foundation model (11B parameters, 200,000+ hours of unlabeled video, 30,000 hours of filtered 2D-platformer gameplay) with a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, MaskGIT-style masked autoregressive dynamics, and an unsupervised latent action model that infers actions without labels [9][38]. Its successors push real-time performance and long-horizon consistency: Genie 2 is an autoregressive latent diffusion model producing consistent worlds for up to ~1 minute [39], while Genie 3 generates dynamic 720p worlds at 20–24 FPS from text prompts with visual memory up to ~1 minute back [40].

The third family treats next-frame prediction as iterative denoising. Sora is a text-conditional diffusion transformer operating on spacetime patches of video/image latent codes, showing emergent 3D consistency, object permanence and digital-world simulation from scale alone, while failing on some basic physics such as glass shattering [11]. DIAMOND replaces discrete latents with a diffusion world model to preserve visual detail, reaching 1.46 mean human-normalized score on Atari 100k and acting as a standalone interactive neural game engine for Counter-Strike: GO [12]. GameNGen turns a diffusion model into a real-time DOOM engine — trained in two phases (an RL agent records sessions, then a diffusion model predicts the next frame conditioned on past frames and actions) — running at 20 FPS on a single TPU with next-frame PSNR 29.4, where human raters are only slightly better than random at distinguishing game clips from simulation after 5 minutes of autoregressive generation [13]. Oasis combines a ViT-based spatial autoencoder with a DiT latent diffusion backbone, trained with Diffusion Forcing to generate frames autoregressively from keyboard input at 20 FPS with a released 500M-parameter checkpoint [14]. UniSim learns a "universal simulator" as a video diffusion observation-prediction model with a unified action interface, rolled out autoregressively and used to train policies that transfer zero-shot to the real world [15].

A fourth, non-generative line is the JEPA family. I-JEPA predicts the representations of target blocks (produced by an EMA target encoder) from a single context block, applying the loss in embedding space rather than pixel/token space with an asymmetric encoder/predictor to prevent collapse [16]. V-JEPA 2 is a 1.2B-parameter video JEPA world model with an encoder + predictor, pre-trained with mask-denoising masked latent feature prediction on a dataset comprising over 1M hours of internet video, reaching 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2 and 39.7 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100; post-training a latent action-conditioned world model (V-JEPA 2-AC) on under 62 hours of unlabeled Droid robot video enables zero-shot planning on Franka arms [17]. JEPA-style objectives deliberately predict only the predictable aspects of a scene (e.g. object trajectories) while ignoring unpredictable pixel detail, in contrast to pixel-level video-generation objectives [17].

Objectives therefore diverge correspondingly: reconstruction plus latent KL dynamics with free bits and stop-gradients (Dreamer family) [6]; joint-embedding prediction plus TD-learning without reconstruction (TD-MPC2) [37]; cross-entropy over discretized tokens (IRIS, Genie, GAIA-1) [8][9][10]; denoising diffusion (DIAMOND, GameNGen, Oasis, Sora, UniSim) [12][13][14][11][15]; and masked target-embedding prediction with EMA encoders (I-JEPA, V-JEPA 2) [16][17]. A convergent decomposition across all families is (i) a discrete or continuous latent representation, (ii) an autoregressive or diffusion dynamics model, and (iii) optionally an action interface supplied by ground-truth actions, language, or a learned latent action model [9].

## Benchmarks and Evaluation Methodology

Evaluation of world models splits along two largely non-overlapping traditions. Model-based RL evaluates agents chiefly on sample efficiency and final return under tight interaction budgets — Atari 100k (26 games, ~400k frames), DeepMind Control Suite, Crafter, DMLab-30, BSuite and Minecraft [18][19]. Here the evidence for sample-efficiency gains is strong and consistent: EfficientZero reaches 194.3% mean and 109.0% median human-normalized score on Atari 100k [18]; DreamerV3 reaches 112% mean / 49% median while setting state of the art on Crafter and three continuous-control suites, and beats IMPALA on DMLab-30 with roughly 130× fewer interactions, with performance improving monotonically with model size [7]; Simulus and TWISTER push Atari 100k to 162% mean human-normalized score via intrinsic motivation, prioritized replay, regression-as-classification and action-conditioned CPC [19]. TD-MPC2 shows the complementary axis, outperforming SAC, DreamerV3 and CURL across 104 continuous-control tasks with one hyperparameter set and beating DreamerV3 on DMControl Humanoid/Dog, though DreamerV3 remains stronger on visual/open-world games [37]. Crafter is used to probe exploration and long-term reasoning, and curiosity-based prioritized replay (Curious Replay) further surpasses prior state of the art there [41].

Generative-video world models inherit a different regime centered on distribution-level and per-frame fidelity: FVD (Fréchet Video Distance with I3D features), PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS [20]. The original FVD study found it agreed with human judgment far better than PSNR/SSIM [20], but follow-up work exposes a strong content bias — FVD is nearly blind to temporal/motion corruption and can be lowered by sampling static or resampled clips, so it rewards appearance over the dynamics a world model is supposed to learn [21]. This critique generalizes: perceptual scores correlate weakly with downstream planning or control, and Physics-IQ shows realism and physical competence are statistically uncorrelated [26][22]. The field's response has been a wave of capability-decomposing benchmarks. WorldMark (500 standardized cases evaluated across ten heterogeneous models in its updated version, using a shared WASD-style action vocabulary) finds visual quality is *not* a proxy for controllability: the model with the best perceptual and aesthetic quality ranks last in translational direction accuracy and latency, exposing a trade-off between responsiveness and stability [23]. WBench (289 cases, 1,058 turns, five dimensions, 22 metrics) evaluates 20–43 models and finds no model dominates across video quality, setting adherence, interaction adherence, consistency and physics compliance [24]. WorldArena (16 perceptual metrics plus Data Engine / Policy Evaluator / Action Planner tasks) reveals a "significant perception–functionality gap": high visual quality does not imply embodied task ability, and world models used as policy evaluators over-report success relative to the RoboTwin simulator [25]. Their shared finding is that strong visual generators can rank last on controllability and latency, and no single interactive model dominates all dimensions [23][24][25].

## Long-Horizon Consistency and Failure Modes

Long-horizon consistency is the recurring technical failure. Autoregressive video/latent rollouts compound small one-step errors into unconstrained generative drift, structural collapse and visual degradation [42]. Video-based dynamics models "lack explicit 3D spatial structure and suffer from geometrically inconsistent long-term rollouts with compounding errors" [43]. Diagnostically, imagined rollouts can be "kinematic, not dynamic": an imagined kinematic-consistency error stays flat while policy rewards collapse across friction boundaries, showing models fit motion kinematics without the underlying dynamics [29]. Mitigations fall into three classes: cycle-consistency/reverse-prediction regularizers (Cycle-World), exposure-bias correction from the model's own rollouts, and structural/3D representations that reduce geometric drift (3D point world models) [42][43].

Hallucination is now treated as a measurable, coverage-dependent phenomenon. A large-scale study (427-hour, 210-task dataset with a 350M-parameter world model) identifies perceptual, action-marginalized and scene-diverging hallucination modes, and shows coverage-aware sampling plus hallucination-predictor curiosity rewards can adapt a world model to unseen environments with as few as 50 real trajectories [44]. A key nuance from the planner-side diagnosis is that long-horizon failure is not always the predictor's fault: with a good latent state, a mis-specified planning cost (squared latent distance) saturates and inverts, so a more accurate predictor can even be a worse planner; replacing the cost lifts long-horizon goal-reaching from 26.0% to 98.0% [28].

Failure modes also cluster around exploitation and objective mismatch. A decision-making-centric position argues the field re-encounters "objective mismatch" — that predictive accuracy is often a poor proxy for control utility, an error the model-based RL literature had already identified — and contrasts this with common visual-realism metrics (FVD/PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, treated in the paper as unreliable proxies for control) and with the near-absence of an reported exploitability gap, the discrepancy between model-predicted and real value for policies optimized against the model [22]. WorldArena shows policy evaluators built on world models systematically over-report success relative to a simulator, plausibly from partial overfitting to successful trajectories [25]. Rollout horizons stay short, compute is heavy, and long-horizon memory is the weakest measured dimension [23].

## Applications: Driving, Interactive Worlds, Robotics, and Science

Across application domains, autonomous driving and games are the two most mature "playable world model" frontiers. In driving, Wayve's GAIA-1 casts world modeling as unsupervised next-token prediction over vector-quantized video tokens with text and action conditioning [10], and GAIA-2 is a domain-specialized latent diffusion world model (8.4B parameters, flow matching) generating up to five spatiotemporally consistent 448×960 camera streams with structured conditioning on ego-actions, agents, weather and road semantics for edge-case/safety-critical scenario synthesis [45]. The DriveDreamer line converges on the same recipe, with DriveDreamer-2 adding an LLM interface that converts user queries into agent trajectories and HD maps for customized driving video generation [46][47]. This is a "data augmentation and scenario synthesis" application: the world model generates training/evaluation data offboard rather than controlling the vehicle [45].

In interactive environments, the trajectory runs Genie (11B, unsupervised from 2D platformer video, latent actions) [9] → Genie 2 (autoregressive latent diffusion, playable 3D worlds from a single image prompt, emergent object interactions and physics, up to ~1 minute of consistency) [39] → Genie 3 (real-time 720p at 20–24 FPS with visual memory up to ~1 minute, shipped as the Project Genie prototype) [40][48]. Parallel efforts target the same real-time controllability: GameNGen renders DOOM at 20 FPS on one TPU with PSNR 29.4 [13], and Decart/Etched's Oasis generates Minecraft-like worlds from keyboard input at 20 FPS with a 500M-parameter open checkpoint, though reviewers note "dream-like" hallucination and roughly 5-minute play limits [14]. World Labs' Marble extends the generative-world line to multimodal 3D scene creation [49].

The most consequential frontier is world models as a path to generalist/embodied agents. LeCun's position paper argues that a configurable, hierarchical predictive world model trained self-supervised in abstract representation space (non-generative, avoiding pixel reconstruction) is the main path to machines that learn, plan and reason like animals, calling world models "the main obstacle toward real progress in AI" [50]. V-JEPA 2 delivers a concrete zero-shot robot-planning demonstration, with Meta releasing physical-reasoning benchmarks (e.g. CausalVQA) where humans at 85–95% still far outperform top models [17][51]. Object-centric and platform efforts (FOCUS; Genie Envisioner) push world models into robotic manipulation [52][53]. Complicating the "world model implies capable agent" narrative, a diagnostic study finds five strong visual world-model agents in Atari Pong contain weak, visibly failing world models — an agent–world-model gap [54].

Scientific applications broaden the claim that learned simulators are general-purpose. AI weather forecasting is arguably the most validated success: TC-Next leverages GraphCast fields to cut cyclone track error by 15–44% and intensity error several-fold [55]. World models are also being applied to spacecraft astrodynamics and other physical simulation, showing the paradigm's reach beyond games and driving [55]. The Critique of World Model sharpens the conceptual debate, arguing a world model is "not fundamentally about generating videos, but about serving as a sandbox for reasoning and thought-experiment", critiquing over-reliance on visual generation and proposing a stateful architecture with a Physical/Agentic/Nested outlook [56].

## Trends and Open Problems

Three trends dominate the last two years. First, the field has consolidated into a small number of architectural recipes and is scaling them: latent state-space models became configuration-robust general algorithms [6][7], token-autoregressive models became foundation models trained on internet-scale video [9][38][36], and diffusion models became real-time interactive engines [13][14]. Second, evaluation is shifting from pixel fidelity toward action-controllability and decision quality, motivated by the discovery that FVD-class metrics are content-biased [21] and that a perception–functionality gap separates realism from utility [23][24][25][22]. Third, world models have spread from RL benchmarks into driving, robotics, games and scientific simulation [45][17][55][49].

Several debates remain unresolved. (a) *Is scaling video generation equal to world modelling?* OpenAI frames scaling video diffusion transformers as a path to "world simulators" while conceding failures on basic physics [11], but a controlled physical-law study finds perfect in-distribution generalization and failure out-of-distribution, with case-based rather than rule-based generalization (feature priority colour > size > velocity > shape) and no improvement from scaling parameters or data — concluding "scaling alone is insufficient for video generation models to uncover fundamental physical laws" [27]. Physics-IQ similarly reports that the best models score only around 24–30 against a 100 physical-variance baseline, with physical understanding found to be unrelated to visual realism [26]. (b) *Are world models necessary for intelligence?* LeCun and the Critique line treat them as central [50][56], while the agent–world-model gap shows strong agents can rest on weak models [54], and philosophical critique argues the world-model lens is unfalsifiable since any failure can be patched by enriching state representations [57]. (c) *Grounding:* structured-interface work warns 2D pixel interfaces hallucinate in unobserved regions and drift once objects leave the frame, and unconstrained self-evolution can produce "causal hallucinations" — internally consistent but physically impossible — while no task-agnostic analogue of FVD exists for physical/causal correctness [58]. (d) *Data, compute and safety:* position work argues scaling data, model and test-time compute is not viable given finite human data (with public-text exhaustion forecast around 2028–2032), synthetic-data model collapse, and energy/water costs, advocating counterfactual simulation with a bounded counterfactual budget and safeguards against acting on harmful imagined outcomes [59].

The methodological prescription emerging across the evaluation literature is consistent: evaluate world models for interventional/action-controllability and closed-loop decision quality rather than pixel fidelity, and report diagnostics — perception–functionality gap, exploitability gap, and long-horizon memory — that current perceptual metrics conceal [21][22][23][25]. Whether the field converges on a standardized definition and benchmark suite, as several 2025–2026 surveys urge [3][4][30], will largely determine whether the "world model" framing matures into a measurable science or remains, as critics charge, an unfalsifiable umbrella [57][56].
